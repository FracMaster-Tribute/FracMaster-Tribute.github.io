FRACMASTERCANADIAN FRACMASTER LTD. · CALGARY

The Calgary crew that took the frac to Siberia

Canadian Fracmaster Ltd. grew from a Western Canadian well-stimulation contractor — cementing, acidizing, fracturing — into a billion-dollar international company that pioneered Western joint ventures in the Soviet Union. Then, in eighteen hard months, it was gone. This page is for the hands who wore the green coveralls.

The story

Well stimulation, done the Alberta way

Fracmaster was incorporated federally on 5 April 1976 as Canadian Fracmasters Limited, renamed Canadian Fracmaster Ltd. on 21 November 1977. From Calgary it provided cementing, acidizing, fracturing and well services to the oilpatch — injecting material at high pressure down a well bore to crack the formation and bring more oil and gas to surface. Headquarters ended up at 1700 Fracmaster Tower, 355 – 4th Avenue S.W., Calgary.

The work

Cementing · Acidizing · Fracturing

The Calgary Herald's summary: “Founded in 1976, Fracmaster rose to great heights providing cementing, acidizing, fracturing and well services to the oilpatch.” It also ran a coiled-tubing business (Flextube) and held coiled-tubing and fracture-treatment patents.

The reach

Canada, the U.S., Russia, China

Western Canada first, then South Texas (Laredo), Louisiana and New Mexico — and, from 1989, joint ventures in Siberia and a 720-well programme in Shandong, China. By May 1998 it claimed roughly 10% of the U.S. pressure-pumping market.

The colours

Red, yellow & green

The livery on this page comes straight off the gear: red FRACMASTER wordmark, yellow trucks, hats and tools, and the dark green coveralls with red shoulder panels and yellow reflective stripes the crews wore from Alberta to Siberia.

People

The names in the record

Alfred Balm — founder & chairman

The Globe and Mail calls Balm “Fracmaster Ltd.'s founder”; the Financial Post called him “the Geneva-based billionaire who built Fracmaster Ltd.” He was chairman and CEO until he sold his 67.5% stake in September 1997 — the hinge event of the whole story.

Ron Bullen — president

President from 1976, per a biographical directory (a weaker source — treat with care), and named as president by Oil & Gas Journal in March 1991 when the Soviet venture turned a profit. The University of Alberta's engineering magazine profiled him in 2007 as “The Master of Frac.”

Doug Ramsay — president to 1994

Served as president of Canadian Fracmaster until 1994 after 12 years building its worldwide presence. In 1999 he co-founded Calfrac Well Services — Fracmaster's direct living legacy.

Source: Calfrac news release, 14 May 2013.

Les Margetak — president & CEO at the end

President by June 1997 and president/CEO at the NYSE listing on 8 April 1998. An April 1998 Financial Post profile described the 44-year-old guiding a firm with ~C$500 million in annual revenue and 1,000 employees worldwide.

Honest gap: no source found names the co-founders, the first yard, or how Balm and Bullen met. Whether they founded it together is not established, so this page doesn't claim it.

Russia & China

First into Siberia

On 13 January 1987 the USSR opened the door to joint ventures. Fracmaster walked through it early — and for a while, it worked brilliantly.

30 March 1989

Uganskfracmaster

The first venture: 51% Uganskneftegas Production Association, 49% Canadian Fracmaster (Offshore) Ltd. and Shell. Over 50 fracturing jobs were done by June 1990, with substantially increased production in treated wells.

Source: OnePetro PETSOC-90-23 (Hussin & Petryk, 1990).

March 1991

Turning a profit

Oil & Gas Journal reported the venture had recouped its investment in two years. Fracmaster stimulated wells at its own expense for a share of the production increase — gains of ~10,000 barrels a day, sold for hard currency. It had invested ~US$23 million and donated over US$5 million to Nefteyugansk and nearby communities.

29 July 1991

Samotlor with PanCanadian

Samotlor PanCanadian Fracmaster Services: 25% PanCanadian, 25% Fracmaster, 50% Samotlorneft — Tyumen region, western Siberia, targeting 35,000 b/d within five years. EBRD loans followed in 1992, and by July 1997 Fracmaster held 50% of Samotlor Services, with gross production over 15,000 b/d.

1996 – 1997

China: 720 wells

A C$50 million deal with China National Petroleum Corp. in Shandong province — up to 720 wells, a C$10 million truck fleet sent in late 1994, and final approval for the operational phase on 12 June 1997.

Rise & fall

From a billion dollars to fifty cents

Shares began trading in fall 1993 (symbol CFC), then listed on the NYSE on 8 April 1998 as FMA. In fall 1997 the company was worth more than C$1 billion. Then came the one-two punch: Russia's currency crisis in August 1998 and oil below US$11 a barrel that December.

Profit and loss, C$ millions

Reported results: 1996 and 1997 profits, then the 1998 loss — which included a C$126 million write-down of Russian assets. Source: company results as reported by the Globe and Mail / Financial Post, 1999.

The instalment that failed

Balm's September 1997 sale was paid in instalments: C$9.75 a share up front, C$9.75 due a year later. On 9 September 1998, holders of over two-thirds of the receipts defaulted, leaving Balm holding roughly 40–43% again — and suing for the balance. A week later Fracmaster put itself up for sale. No binding bids came.

CCAA protection — March 1999

Protection under the Companies' Creditors Arrangement Act was granted 18 March 1999 (some reports say 19 March — the dates conflict in the record, so both are noted). Debt topped C$130 million. Arthur Andersen Inc. was monitor, then receiver/manager.

Sold to BJ Services

After a contested court fight with UTI Energy, Calfrac and Balm's group, Justice Paperny approved the sale to BJ Services of Houston on 21 May 1999. It closed 28 June 1999 for US$78.5 million total. The Alberta Court of Appeal dismissed all four appeals on 9 June 1999 (UTI Energy Corp. v. Fracmaster Ltd., 1999 ABCA 178). Shareholders got nothing.

The Calgary Herald, 8 June 1999: the sale meant “the end of the 23-year-old Fracmaster name from the Canadian oilpatch” — “the technology and expertise it has will join BJ Services.” The share price told the same story: a 52-week high of C$20, a last trade of C$0.50.

Timeline

1976 – 1999, and after

  1. 5 Apr 1976

    Incorporated. Canadian Fracmasters Limited, federally incorporated in Calgary; office at 335 8th Ave SW. Renamed Canadian Fracmaster Ltd. on 21 Nov 1977.

  2. 30 Mar 1989

    First Soviet joint venture. Uganskfracmaster formed with Uganskneftegas and Shell to stimulate wells in Soviet oil and gas fields.

  3. 25 Mar 1991

    Profit in Siberia. Oil & Gas Journal: the venture has recouped its investment; ~10,000 b/d of production gains.

  4. 29 Jul 1991

    Samotlor. Samotlor PanCanadian Fracmaster Services announced for the Tyumen region of western Siberia.

  5. Fall 1993

    Public company. Shares begin trading on the Toronto, Montreal and Alberta exchanges, symbol CFC.

  6. 9 Sep 1997

    The Balm sale. Alfred Balm sells his 67.5% stake by instalment receipts (~C$563 million total). Market value tops C$1 billion that fall.

  7. 8 Apr 1998

    NYSE listing. Fracmaster lists in New York as FMA; ~1,000 employees worldwide; 1997 profit C$43.4 million.

  8. Aug – Dec 1998

    The crash. Russia's currency crisis; oil below US$11/bbl. The instalment payment fails 9 September. 1998 loss: C$137.3 million.

  9. 18 Mar 1999

    CCAA protection. Court protection granted; Arthur Andersen appointed monitor. Over 250 jobs had already been cut.

  10. 21 May 1999

    BJ Services wins. Court approves the BJ sale (US$80 million headline). Appeals dismissed 9 June 1999 — 1999 ABCA 178; FTC clearance the same day.

  11. 28 Jun 1999

    Closed — and reborn. BJ Services completes the purchase (US$78.5 million). The same day, ex-Fracmaster people co-found Calfrac Well Services.

  12. 2 Jul 2001

    Struck off. The Alberta corporate shell is struck from the registry (unofficial index — verify officially). The name lives on in Russia as ZAO Samotlor Fracmaster Services into the late 2000s.

Legacy

What carried on

Calfrac Well Services

Founded 28 June 1999 — the very day the BJ sale closed — by Ronald P. Mathison, Douglas Ramsay, Gordon Dibb and Robert Roberts. Ramsay had run Fracmaster until 1994. The Canadian fracturing business, in new colours, carried straight on.

BJ Services → Baker Hughes

Fracmaster's technology, expertise and Russian operations joined BJ Services, later part of Baker Hughes. In Siberia, ZAO Samotlor Fracmaster Services kept the name working for TNK-BP into the late 2000s.

The group & the gear

The name never really left the patch: the Facebook group still trades photos and stories, and the buckles, caps and yellow-handled tools still turn up in kitchens, garages and museum cases — like the Canadian Energy Museum's knife, playing cards and well tree caps in the gallery above.

Open questions this page won't guess at

The founding story (co-founders, first yard, first customers) is not in any source found. The “Fracmaster Tower” building history — when it got the name, who built it — is unverified; only the address is confirmed. No dedicated Fracmaster fonds was found at Glenbow / University of Calgary Archives, Alberta On Record or the Provincial Archives of Alberta — that's a negative search result, not proof nothing exists; the Glenbow live search was blocked by a CAPTCHA. Figures that conflict in the record (CCAA date 18 vs 19 March 1999; employee counts of 1,000 / 667 / ~630 at different dates) are shown with their dates rather than smoothed over.
